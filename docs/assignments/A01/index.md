## Objective
Task A — Portfolio Analysis

Portfolio: Noah DiMenna — MEGR 2156 Design Portfolio

Navigability.
Noah DiMenna's portfolio is easy to move through because each assignment has its own section in the navigation menu. Someone like me can go directly to assignments A1 through A11 without having to search through the whole website. The A1 page is also divided into sections, which makes it easier to find specific information. I think a visitor looking for a certain assignment would be able to find it in less than 60 seconds.

Reproductiblity.
The portfolio gives the reader an idea of what was done, but there is not enough information to completely reproduce the work. Some of the engineering ideas and project details are explained, but more calculations, dimensions, materials, drawings, and step-by-step information are needed. I could understand the general idea behind the work. 

Evidence of Reasoning.
The portfolio is made up around the ideas of Analyze, Decide, and Communicate, so it encourages the student to explain the reasoning behind the work instead of only showing a regular answer.  Adding more calculations, comparisons, and explanations of why certain decisions were made would make the engineering reasoning stronger.


Professional Tone.
The website is organized in a way that would make sense to a professor, another engineering student, or an employer. The writing explains the purpose of the work instead of only showing pictures. I think the writing could be improved by using more specific engineering terms and numbers when explaining why a design works or does not work.

Portfolio 2
Portfolio: J. McAdam — Design Projects Portfolio
Navigability. 
J. McAdam's portfolio is also easy to navigate. The menu separates the homepage, About Me section, Portfolio Overview, and assignments A1 through A11. The A1 page has another menu that breaks the assignment into smaller sections. For example, I can go directly to the fuel pump analysis instead of scrolling through the entire page. Because of this setup, I think a visitor could find a specific assignment or section in less than 60 seconds.

Reproducibility. 
This portfolio gives someone more technical information about the work. In the BMW E30 fuel pump analysis, there are specifications such as pressure, flow rate, current, and operating temperature. It also explains some of the materials, geometry, and manufacturing methods used in the product. Even with this information, a person probably could not build an exact copy of the fuel pump using only the portfolio. However, the information provided gives the reader a strong understanding of how the product works.

Evidence of Reasoning.
One thing that stood out to me was how the portfolio explains why certain design choices were made. For example, the fuel pump analysis explains why the pump is located inside the fuel tank and connects that choice to cooling and pump performance. It also talks about manufacturing choices and how they affect the final product. 

Professional Tone.
The portfolio uses engineering terms, numbers, equations, and pictures to explain the work. This makes it useful for someone with an engineering background because they can see the technical reasoning behind the design.
