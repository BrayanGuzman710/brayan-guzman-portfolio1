As I gained more experience with engineering, I became interested not only in fixing existing systems but also in learning how they are designed.

My experience with mechatronics has allowed me to work with both mechanical and electrical systems. I have gained experience with areas such as automation, manufacturing, troubleshooting, maintenance, and engineering design. I have also worked with engineering software including SolidWorks, Creo, and AutoCAD. These experiences have helped me understand how the concepts learned in class connect to equipment and problems in the real world.

Mechanical engineering interests me because it gives me the opportunity to continue building my knowledge of machines, materials, manufacturing, and mechanical design. I am becoming an engineer who likes to combine hands-on experience with engineering analysis. I do not want to only know how to calculate an answer. I want to understand what the answer means.

As I continue my education, I want to improve my ability to design mechanical systems, solve problems, and make decisions based on engineering evidence. My goal is to become an engineer who can take a problem, understand what is causing it, develop possible solutions, and explain why the final solution is the right choice.

What Does It Mean to Defend an Engineering Decision — and Do I Currently Know How to Do It?

To me, defending an engineering decision means being able to explain why I made a certain choice and having evidence to support it. That evidence could include calculations, testing, and comparing different solutions. I think I understand the basic idea of defending an engineering decision, but I still have room to improve. Sometimes I know that a solution will work, but I need to get better at explaining exactly why it is the best choice and supporting that decision with engineering evidence. By the end of this semester, I want to be more confident in making engineering decisions and explaining my reasoning so that another engineer can understand my work.
